## Implications for legacy routes

Six existing Residential specialist routes remain diagnostic/reuse/migration review items. The map gives them no independent competing primary cluster. Six illustrative neutral-service routes express the recommended semantic roles, but **must not be created alongside legacy duplicates**. After approval, compare retaining/repositioning an existing object/path against a new route and any necessary transition. No redirect/merge/deletion is approved here.

A useful Residential hub can still help homeowners choose a service; a commercial SEO tree need not imply a symmetric Residential SEO tree. Generic pages should satisfy homeowner needs and explain business routing without pretending every searcher belongs to one audience. Explicit commercial specialists must add confirmed property contacts, access/tenant constraints, affected operating areas, scheduling and business-item considerations.

GSC joined water queries are diagnostic only: generic water-Chicago appears on Homepage323 impressions/48.4 and Residential Water83/63.3; commercial water-Chicago on Commercial Water42/45.6, guide2/55.0 and Residential Water1/74.0. These averages do not establish the correct future owner, causal cannibalization or static current ranks; page aggregations need not sum to property totals. Preserve useful existing visibility while evaluating the market-defined role. Informational guides should answer their question and link the transactional owner, not become another sales duplicate.

## Content and approval gate

1. Start with the market task and approved cluster ID, then select the implementation object/route.
2. Assign one primary owner per intent; supporting terminology can overlap naturally.
3. Keep hubs concise and navigable; specialists answer the full task; narrow supporting pages explain their distinct job.
4. Require distinct evidence and useful copy for audience-specific pages; no automatically cloned audience/service or service/city grids.
5. Update affected map/brief/link recommendations together before changing ownership.
6. Obtain separate approval for implementation and any URL transitions; compare consistent query/page outcomes afterward.
