## Finding

The rollout filled the service inventory, but most pages are still scope-and-enquiry outlines rather than fully useful explanations of the service. Their biggest shared defect is **describing a conversation about work instead of explaining the work**. This is independent of word count. Water514 already answers many practical questions and needs targeted additions; Fire517 and Mold516 need substantive service-process explanations. The narrower Board-Up488 is sufficient for its limited temporary-protection role. Of17 pages,1 is sufficient,8 need targeted expansion and8 need substantial revision.

The remaining substantial revisions are contents521, Commercial Mold519, General Contracting522, Odor489, Moisture Mapping490 and Dehumidification491. Their core offer, actual process or deliverable remains unclear. Commercial Water515, Fire520, Reconstruction524 and Contents525 have real audience differences—authority, premises access, ownership and operating constraints—but need confirmed practical detail rather than more audience labels. Reconstruction518, Storm523 and Extraction487 need focused explanations and handoffs.

All17 contain six relevant FAQ answers, counted once even when collapsed. Their existence is not evidence of sufficient coverage: many answers defer to a conversation, deny a guarantee or repeat scope limits. Those limits should stay accurate while more useful positive explanations replace repetitive questions.

## Ownership, evidence and scope boundaries

The latest rollout record explicitly documents owner approval of the October22 ownership recommendations; the earlier research’s “pending approval” labels are historical. Preserve514/517/516 as generic Water/Fire/Mold owners on their existing Residential routes;518/523/521 as specialist owners;515/519 as commercial-specific owners;520/524/525 as commercial support;522 as a distinct planned-project scope;487–491 as narrower tasks. No new root synonyms, symmetric audience tree, new city pages, merging or redirects are recommended.

GCPRO packs and restores belongings. It does **not** perform mold testing or post-remediation testing. Storage and return logistics, specialist item recovery, particular equipment, commercial containment/phasing, response times, insurance handling and professional/permit roles require owner facts where not already confirmed. Competitor claims are prompts for fact collection, not evidence of GCPRO capability. No proposed addition authorizes a website change.

Real comparison photographs are useful visible proof but do not establish cause, full work scope, duration, credentials or commercial operating procedures. Mold’s Glenview water pair remains water evidence; the attic pair is the relevant mold example. Entrance/clubroom work must not be relabeled loss reconstruction without actual job facts.
