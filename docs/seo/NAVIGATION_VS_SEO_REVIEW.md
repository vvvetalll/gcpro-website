## Recommended navigation approach

1. **Preserve both audience parents and all six core service choices in each.** Residential keeps Water, Fire/Smoke, Mold, Reconstruction, Storm and Personal Property; Commercial keeps Water, Fire/Smoke, Mold, Reconstruction, General Contracting and Personal Property. Their different sixth service reflects customer tasks, not a requirement to equalize SEO trees. Reordering or label refinements remain proposals.
2. **Use one useful destination per task where possible.** Residential choices can reach the neutral service roles. Commercial Water/Mold favor specific destinations; Commercial Fire/Reconstruction remain conditional on meaningful business content. Personal Property is the clearest candidate for the same destination in both menus. A shared URL appearing twice is not itself duplicate content or keyword cannibalization.
3. **Keep the selected audience understandable after the click.** A shared page needs an honest cross-audience title, readily visible home/business guidance and relevant enquiry options. Keep breadcrumbs consistent with the page's actual hierarchy; do not disguise a neutral page as a Commercial-only page. A neutral page under a legacy Residential path has a perception/breadcrumb tradeoff requiring explicit review; root-level URLs are not automatically necessary.
4. **Make supporting services discoverable without turning the menus into a technical catalog.** Retain useful shortcuts; consider lower visual prominence for drying/assessment processes and contextual access from relevant Commercial content. Do not automatically copy all five Residential support entries into Commercial or introduce more pages.
5. **Resolve reuse before changing links.** Approve the roles, exact destination objects, audience presentation and any separate migration plan together. Do not point menus to † routes today, create neutral pages alongside near-duplicate legacy pages, or use canonical/noindex settings as an automatic substitute for a coherent content decision. Existing links and URLs stay unchanged in this review.

### Example journeys after approval

- **Homeowner with a burst pipe:** Residential → Water Damage → approved neutral Water owner → immediate home-recovery guidance and Request Service. Exact object/path still to be chosen.
- **Facilities manager after a sprinkler leak:** Commercial → Water Damage → existing 515 candidate with business coordination and scope; generic information remains available without forcing a homeowner detour.
- **Homeowner or business with damaged contents:** either audience menu → its familiar contents label → the same approved Contents owner, clearly presenting both use cases. No redundant page is needed merely to preserve both menu entries.
- **Business planning an improvement:** Commercial → General Contracting → 522 candidate; Commercial → Reconstruction remains explicitly for rebuilding after damage.
