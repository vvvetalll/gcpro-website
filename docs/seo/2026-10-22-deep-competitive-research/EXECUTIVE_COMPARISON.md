## Selection and market boundaries

The original ten were rechecked against 13 current query observations. Nine remain primary comparators; Excel replaces BELFOR in the primary ten because direct local water visibility and a locally oriented domain make it a more actionable acquisition comparison. BELFOR remains a supplemental enterprise/Contents benchmark: its Northbrook prefix estimates zero, yet its local page occurs in the Contents observation. Midway remains because it is directly present for commercial/contents despite minimal Ahrefs estimates. This cohort balances broad query coverage, service overlap and local applicability; it is not a count of paid placements.

Mold Pro Chicago, Enviro Resources, EHC Industries, Green Home Solutions, Chicago Mold Remediators, Roto-Rooter, Real Restoration, PuroClean, Big Shoulders and the Glenview firms are important query-specific challengers in the SERP review. Their specialist presence informs service briefs; lack of a full primary dossier does not make them irrelevant. Directories/forums are demand/discovery competitors, not restoration providers to copy.

## Where GCPRO can compete

GCPRO already has coherent audience navigation and real local project images. It lacks the complete, proof-backed specialist destinations that make Romexterra's mold/fire pages useful, the contents workflow visible at Restore, and the business-operation detail visible at ATI/ServiceMaster. Improve those precise weaknesses before adding breadth. A smaller site with verified service/process/project detail is a more realistic first step than reproducing a national network or hundreds of local combinations.

No metric here proves why a company ranks. Brand recognition, review history, local proximity, links, page relevance and project experience are plausible combined advantages; isolating their causal contribution would require additional data and experiments. GCPRO's roughly1K referring domains do not negate an authority gap: counts include low-quality links and do not measure local trust.
